# GRC Office — Compliance Management System (CMS), ISMS, AIMS, and GRC

## Overview

This skill codifies the responsibilities of the GRC office covering four interrelated
programs: the Compliance Management System (CMS), the Governance, Risk, and Compliance
(GRC) Management System, the Information Security Management System (ISMS), and the
Artificial Intelligence Management System (AIMS). Use it to plan, monitor, improve, and
control these programs while ensuring effective alignment of people, processes, and
technology.

## When to Apply

- Planning the implementation or integration of CMS and GRC management systems.
- Defining priorities, timelines, responsibilities, and implementation requirements.
- Ensuring alignment among people, processes, and technology across ISMS, AIMS, CMS,
  and GRC initiatives.
- Monitoring GRC project progress, milestones, deliverables, pending actions, and
  stakeholder responsibilities.
- Assessing whether people, processes, and supporting technologies work together
  effectively.
- Identifying ISMS/AIMS improvement opportunities and improving governance, risk, and
  compliance processes, controls, documentation, and coordination.
- Strengthening employee awareness, process efficiency, and effective use of technology.
- Handling ad hoc risk, compliance, and governance tasks.
- Reviewing implementation activities for alignment with organizational requirements
  and applicable standards.
- Supporting corrective actions, risk treatment, compliance controls, and accountability.

## Core Responsibilities

### 1. Planning

- Plan the implementation of the CMS and the GRC Management System.
- Define priorities, timelines, responsibilities, and implementation requirements.
- Ensure alignment among people, processes, and technology across ISMS, AIMS, CMS, and
  GRC initiatives.
- Identify integration points between the management systems to avoid duplication of
  controls and documentation.

### 2. Monitoring

- Monitor and oversee the progress of the GRC project.
- Track milestones, deliverables, pending actions, and stakeholder responsibilities.
- Assess whether people, processes, and supporting technologies are working together
  effectively.
- Escalate risks of delay and follow up with stakeholders to ensure timely progress.

### 3. Improving

- Identify opportunities to improve the ISMS and the AIMS.
- Improve governance, risk, and compliance processes, controls, documentation, and
  coordination.
- Strengthen employee awareness, process efficiency, and the effective use of
  technology.

### 4. Controlling

- Handle ad hoc tasks related to governance, risk, and compliance.
- Review implementation activities to ensure compliance with organizational
  requirements and applicable standards.
- Support corrective actions, risk treatment, compliance controls, and accountability.
- Maintain effective control by ensuring proper alignment of people, processes, and
  technology.

## The People-Process-Technology Lens

Apply this lens across all four responsibility areas:

- **People:** clear roles, responsibilities, accountability, and awareness.
- **Process:** defined, efficient, documented, and auditable workflows and controls.
- **Technology:** supporting tools that enable, not obstruct, compliance work.

Before concluding any plan, review, or improvement, ask whether people, processes, and
technology are aligned and whether any one of the three is a gap.

## Operating Standards and Frameworks

Reference the applicable standards when planning and reviewing, and always confirm
which are in scope for the organization:

- **ISMS:** ISO/IEC 27001 (and ISO/IEC 27002 for controls)
- **AIMS:** ISO/IEC 42001 (AI management systems)
- **CMS / GRC:** ISO 37301 (Compliance Management Systems), COSO, NIST frameworks
  (e.g., NIST AI RMF), and organizational internal policies
- Check whether the organization is pursuing certification, attestation, or internal
  compliance maturity as the target outcome.

## Workflow

### Plan a program or initiative

1. Clarify scope, objectives, and applicable standards.
2. Identify required controls, artifacts, and stakeholders.
3. Define priorities, timelines, responsibilities, and implementation requirements.
4. Sequence work into phases with owners, dependencies, and due dates.
5. Check alignment of people, processes, and technology for each phase.
6. Produce a plan summary covering priorities, timeline, and coordination requirements.

### Monitor progress

1. Review the current status of activities, milestones, and deliverables.
2. Compare actual progress against the plan.
3. Identify pending actions, overdue items, and stakeholder responsibilities.
4. Assess how effectively people, processes, and supporting technologies work together.
5. Recommend or initiate stakeholder follow-ups and escalation.

### Improve a management system

1. Assess current controls, processes, and documentation against the target standard.
2. Identify gaps and improvement opportunities (ISMS, AIMS, and GRC processes).
3. Consider employee awareness, process efficiency, and technology effectiveness.
4. Prioritize improvements by risk and effort.
5. Propose changes with clear rationale and documentation.

### Control and review

1. Triage ad hoc risk, compliance, and governance requests.
2. Review implementation activities for alignment with requirements and standards.
3. Support risk treatment, corrective action plans, and accountability.
4. Ensure documentation is complete, current, and auditable.
5. Confirm alignment of people, processes, and technology before closing out.

## Deliverables

Typical outputs produced under this skill:

- Implementation plans with priorities, timelines, responsibilities, and coordination
  requirements
- Status reports and progress tracking (milestones, deliverables, pending actions,
  stakeholder responsibilities)
- Alignment assessments across people, processes, and technology
- Gap analyses and improvement recommendations (ISMS, AIMS, GRC)
- Risk treatment and corrective action plans
- Compliance and governance documentation (policies, controls, evidence)

## Reminders

- Always confirm the organization's scope, applicable standards, and target outcomes
  before assuming requirements.
- Prefer auditable, traceable documentation: every control or action should map to a
  requirement and evidence.
- Distinguish between the four programs (ISMS, AIMS, CMS, GRC) and keep their scope
  boundaries clear while coordinating their integration.
- Keep the people-process-technology lens front of mind in every plan, review, and
  improvement.
- When tracking progress, keep one source of truth (register, tracker, or tool) and
  update it consistently.
